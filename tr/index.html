Gold vs. the Fed's balance sheet

Sources and basis

Sources: Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System (US); U.S. Department of the Treasury; Kitco.

Calculated figures, not balance-sheet items. The Federal Reserve does not own gold. The gold belongs to the U.S. Treasury (261,498,926 troy oz, carried at the statutory USD 42.2222 per oz); the Fed holds gold certificates at that rate, shown as “Gold Certificate Account” (about USD 11.04 billion). The figures are calculated by GoldPremium from measured gold prices and published weekly balance-sheet data (Wednesday levels, H.4.1, USD million). “Notes in circulation” is the net figure, after deducting the Reserve Banks’ own holdings. Gold price: the series of the chart above (COMEX futures via Yahoo Finance, GoldPremium; current value: Kitco). GoldPremium.org is not affiliated with the Federal Reserve.

Economic significance

A measure of money-supply coverage: The ratio lets you estimate what an ounce of gold would have to cost in order to theoretically cover a given share of the U.S. cash supply.

Inflation and confidence indicator: A marked rise over longer periods indicates that the gold price is growing faster than physical currency in circulation. This often reflects rising inflation expectations, a loss of confidence in paper currencies, or heavy central-bank gold buying.

Limits

Only part of the money supply: Federal Reserve notes capture physical cash only. They exclude electronic deposits and bank balances (the broader M1 and M2 aggregates), which make up most of today's liquidity.

Not a gold-standard price: To arrive at a calculated “gold standard price” you would divide the money supply by the physical U.S. gold reserves (in ounces), not the current market price of gold by the money supply. That calculation is shown in the “Coverage value” view.

GoldPremium.org Hakkında

2025 sonunda ve Ocak 2026'da fiziksel değerli metal piyasası alışılmadık bir baskı altına girdi. Altın rekorlar kırdı — 1980'den bu yana en güçlü Ocak ayı — gümüşte ise gerçek bir fiziksel kıtlık yaşandı ve bölgesel primler keskin biçimde ayrıştı. Bir süre boyunca aynı bir kiloluk külçe, nereden alındığına bağlı olarak belirgin biçimde farklı fiyatlara mal oldu. Burada gösterilen Şanghay gümüş fiyatı, Çin KDV'sini (%13) içerir.

Bu tür anlar, tek bir «dünya spot fiyatı»nın arkasında kolayca gözden kaçar — oysa fiziksel altın ve gümüşün asıl hikâyesi tam da buradadır. GoldPremium.org bu farkları görünür kılmak için yapıldı: dünyanın başlıca piyasalarından — Frankfurt, İstanbul, Şanghay, New York ve daha fazlası — fiziksel alış ve satış fiyatlarını toplar ve yan yana koyar; böylece herkes her piyasanın uluslararası referansın ne kadar üzerinde ya da altında işlem gördüğünü görebilir.

Karşılaştırmayı anlamlı tutmak için mümkün olduğunca 1 kg döküm külçe kullanıyoruz — aynı ürün, aynı şekilde ölçülmüş. Bunun mümkün olmadığı durumlarda (standart, birim veya veri farklılıkları), en yakın karşılaştırılabilir temeli kullanır ve kaynağı belirtiriz.

Satıcılar, 1 kg altın ve gümüş ürünleri için fiyat verilerini (API Ask/Bid) bizimle paylaşmaya davetlidir; katılımcı satıcılar, web sitelerine bağlantıyla birlikte ücretsiz olarak listelenir.

About GoldPremium.org

In late 2025 and January 2026, the physical precious-metals market came under unusual strain. Gold set records — the strongest January since 1980 — while silver went through an outright physical squeeze, and regional premiums diverged sharply. For a time, the same one-kilo bar cost noticeably different amounts depending on where it was bought. The Shanghai silver price shown on this page includes China's VAT (13 %).

Moments like this are easy to miss behind a single headline "world spot price" — yet they are where the real story of physical gold and silver lives. GoldPremium.org was built to make these differences visible: it collects physical buy and sell prices from the world's major markets — Frankfurt, Istanbul, Shanghai, New York and more — and places them side by side, so anyone can see how far each market trades above or below the international benchmark.

To keep the comparison meaningful, we use 1 kg cast bullion bars wherever possible — the same product, measured the same way. Where that isn't available (differing standards, units or data), we use the closest comparable basis and label the source.

Dealers are invited to provide pricing data (API Ask/Bid) for 1 kg gold and silver products; participating dealers are listed with a link to their site, free of charge.

Contact: contact@goldpremium.org
Über GoldPremium.org

Ende 2025 und im Januar 2026 geriet der physische Edelmetallmarkt unter ungewöhnlichen Druck. Gold markierte Rekorde — der stärkste Januar seit 1980 —, bei Silber kam es zu einer regelrechten physischen Knappheit, und die regionalen Aufschläge liefen stark auseinander. Zeitweise kostete derselbe Ein-Kilo-Barren je nach Bezugsort spürbar unterschiedlich viel. Der hier gezeigte Shanghaier Silberkurs enthält die chinesische MwSt. (13 %).

Solche Momente verschwinden leicht hinter einem einzigen „Weltspotpreis" — dabei liegt genau dort die eigentliche Geschichte von physischem Gold und Silber. GoldPremium.org wurde gemacht, um diese Unterschiede sichtbar zu machen: Es sammelt physische An- und Verkaufspreise der wichtigsten Märkte weltweit — Frankfurt, Istanbul, Shanghai, New York und weitere — und stellt sie direkt nebeneinander, sodass jeder sieht, wie weit ein Markt über oder unter der internationalen Benchmark liegt.

Um den Vergleich aussagekräftig zu halten, nutzen wir nach Möglichkeit 1-kg-Gussbarren — dasselbe Produkt, gleich gemessen. Wo das nicht möglich ist (unterschiedliche Standards, Einheiten oder Datenlage), verwenden wir die nächstbeste vergleichbare Basis und kennzeichnen die Quelle.

Händler sind eingeladen, uns Preisdaten (API Ask/Bid) für 1-kg-Gold- und Silberprodukte bereitzustellen; teilnehmende Händler werden inklusive Verlinkung gelistet, kostenfrei.
